# Cyanoacrylate Processes and Platinum Silicone

In General Cured Cyanoacrylate will not prevent silicone cure and will not affect platinum silicone negatively, here are some ideas on how to use it in your mold making and casting journey.

Avoid the use of gels or Cyanoacrylate with other additives as we don't know what those might be, prefer the cheap, watery, pure Cyanoacrylate.

## Mold Smoothing

Using a tube of cyanoacrylate can be used on a mold to smooth it out, say if you have 3D printed a mold and need to get rid of layer lines; platinum silicone will cure over "cured cyanoacrylate" without an issue.

Using cyanoacrylate can prove easier than using something like resin, specially with small parts, also cleaner overall; however you might need many tubes of it, and maybe a couple of layers since it sits thin.

## Feature Glossy Finish.

You can create a glossy effect on a plastic part by using cyanoacrylate, for example, the tooth and inner mouth of a creature after it has been painted.

This will work like a laquer protection, but without affecting the cure.

Note however that gluing silicone to that smooth surface later might prove more difficult, so avoid getting too close to the gums or anything that you plan on gluing silicone later.

## Gluing Platinum Silicone

Cyanoacrylate can serve as aid when gluing platinum silicone to other surfaces like hard plastic and metal, but only if the force to be applied is parallell to the gluing surface; and only if the platinum silicone surface to glue is matte and not glossy.

Trying to see if it works out is usually a non issue as it either works or doesn't causing no damage to the silicone, but it might affect the plastic surface, making it more difficult to bond later with another agent if the gluing attempt fails; which is why I recommend roughening the plastic first, or inserting a piece of mosquito net or some cloth to change the properties.

This method was found and used in puppets that I made that held for years, however the only part of known use was around the skull, in the cheekbones, as they take forces parallell to the gluing surface when opening the mouth.

It is better to also add other adhesives and use the cyanoacrylate only in narrow areas where nothing else fits.

## Surface roughening

You can roughen a surface (to make it easier for platinum silicone to adhere) by using Cyanoacrylate and Sodium bicarbonate (for example the lower part of the jaw of a creature); however platinum silicone will still come off, why do this then?... well now the surface is rough and the silicone
has created a very nice microscopic coupling making it hard to slid, adding then other agents in a thin layer will create a very strong coupling, specially against lateral movement.'

Platinum silicone will cure just fine over the roughened surface.
